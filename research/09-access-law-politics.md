# Access, rights, and politics of the Sakya corpus

## Verdict

- **Physical access (Q1): no.** Foreigners cannot enter the TAR independently. Sakya is a guided tourist stop, not a research library. No 2020–2026 first-hand account was found of a foreign scholar studying the scripture-wall holdings.
- **Who controls the scans (Q2): the monastery and PRC institutions, not the public web.** TAR Sakya digitization is internal. English articles that put those scans under Creative Commons on sakyalibrary.com conflate two projects. National Chinese platforms host a token Tibetan-script set, not the Sakya wall.
- **Copyright (Q3): the words are public domain; the scans and the politics are not.** Age frees the texts. Scan rights, relic law, and CARE ethics cut against treating the corpus as free raw material.
- **Restricted content (Q4): Lamdre and Hevajra practice manuals are the hard no.** Lineage holders have always gated those. 84000 publishes Kangyur tantras openly with a warning; Wisdom and BDRC lock sealed texts.
- **Political risk (Q5): real for anyone inside the PRC.** A Kirti monastery librarian was sentenced in 2024 to three years for importing religious books from India. 2022 rules make unlicensed online religious publishing inside China a licensing offence.
- **Reachability in 2026 (Q6): partly — exile and already-circulating literature, not the TAR wall.** An OSS project can reuse public-domain and CC Sakya texts published outside the PRC. It cannot obtain the monastery’s unpublished scans, and should not scrape PRC databases or machine-publish restricted tantric manuals.

## Findings

### 1. Physical access

[FACT] Sakya Monastery is in Sa'gya County, Shigatse Prefecture, TAR. Foreigners still need a Tibet Travel Permit via a licensed agency, a guide, and a fixed itinerary; independent travel remains banned in 2026. Shigatse/Sakya also needs an Alien’s Travel Permit ([The Land of Snows, Jan 2026](https://www.thelandofsnows.com/tibet-travel-regulations/)). The TAR is the only PRC provincial-level unit that requires foreigners — including diplomats — to request permission to visit. US consular officials made their first official TAR visit since 2019 in September 2025 (five days; tourist sites, not research) ([US Reciprocal Access report, Apr 2026](https://www.state.gov/report-to-congress-on-access-to-tibetan-areas-section-4-of-the-reciprocal-access-to-tibet-act-of-2018-p-l-115-330-8-usc-1182)).

[FACT] Interior photography of the Great Scripture Hall is generally prohibited ([China Dragon Travel](https://www.chinadragontravel.com/tibetan-monastery-photography-etiquette/)). Harvard–Yenching instructor Karma Gongde said in June 2026 of comparable block prints: “If these were in Tibet, they would be under lock and key” ([Harvard FAS](https://current.fas.harvard.edu/stories/harvard-yenching-library-return-tibetan-buddhist-canon)). The nearest research analogue is TAR Sanskrit palm-leaf manuscripts: a 2006–2011 survey produced colour scans and sixty-one portfolio volumes in about five copies. Ernst Steinkellner wrote the treasure “is still out of bounds for scholars, both within China and beyond,” as a political lock ([Steinkellner](https://www.oeaw.ac.at/fileadmin/Institute/IKGA/PDF/digitales/Steinkellner_2016_manuscripts_TAR.pdf)).

### 2. Who controls the scans

[FACT] TAR Sakya digitization is run on site by monks under the monastery management committee. Luo Zhuo Jia Cuo (洛卓加措) told China News Service in 2022 that scanning began in 2011; cataloguing finished by 2020; everything *except* the great scripture wall had been page-scanned — about 10,000 volumes, 20% — with an internal catalogue and full-text database ([TAR Culture Bureau, 13 Mar 2022](https://wlt.xizang.gov.cn/xwzx_69/wlyw/wldt/202203/t20220313_288139.html)). As a CPPCC member in March 2026 he proposed a national “digital twin” special project — a request that only makes sense if those scans are not already public ([China.org.cn, 11 Mar 2026](http://cul.china.com.cn/2026-03/11/content_43370134.htm)). A July 2025 *China Daily* feature quotes monk Kunga Trinley: seven monks, nearly seven years, “nearly 3,000 volumes, approximately 7,000 pages,” figures that cannot be reconciled with 2022 without reporter error ([chinaculture.org, 8 Jul 2025](https://en.chinaculture.org/a/202507/08/WS686c42daa31000e9a573aa44.html)). Neither article says the images are online.

[FACT] **A Sakya Digital Library** at sakyalibrary.com is a different institution: International Buddhist Academy, Kathmandu, funded by the Khenchen Appey Foundation. It republishes IBA/Sachen e-texts under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 ([about](http://sakyalibrary.com/home/page/f344da50-c2c1-452b-a8f7-95ae75e6e7fc)). Lifestyle sites ([My Modern Met, 15 Jun 2024](https://mymodernmet.com/sakya-monastery-library/)) paste that CC licence onto the TAR monastery. The conflation is false.

[FACT] 中华古籍资源库 is free, no-login, and reachable from abroad. In September 2023 it added **60 items across 16 scripts**, Tibetan among them — not a Sakya dump ([NLC](https://www.nlc.cn/pcab/zx/xw/20240103_2637566.shtml)). Tibet Library “cloud sharing” had 23,116 leaves online by April 2021, not the Sakya wall ([Xinhua](http://www.xinhuanet.com/2021-04/23/c_1127367997.htm)). CTRC’s Tibetan literature data centre (~12,000 volumes) launched in 2021 **on the internal network**, “to be opened later” ([CTRC, 22 Jul 2021](http://www.tibetology.ac.cn/2021-07/22/content_41684640.htm)). [SPECULATION] The monastery’s scans sit on local systems. There is no evidence they are in 中华古籍资源库, BDRC, or any open IIIF store.

### 3. Copyright, community rights, digital colonialism

[FACT] The texts are public domain by age. US law gives no new copyright to slavish photographs of two-dimensional public-domain works ([Bridgeman v. Corel, 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeman_v_Corel)); English law reached the same result in *THJ v Sheridan* (2023). PRC Copyright Law (2020) protects original “photographic works” and a right of communication through information networks ([CJO](https://www.chinajusticeobserver.com/law/x/copyright-law-of-china-20201111)). The Cultural Relics Protection Law (effective 1 Mar 2025) classifies ancient manuscripts as movable cultural relics; Sakya is a first-batch national key cultural relic site. Export and unsupervised photography of graded relics are administrative problems, not just copyright ([NPC](http://en.npc.gov.cn.cdurl.cn/2024-11/08/c_1115996.htm)).

[FACT] CARE principles (Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, Ethics; Carroll et al. 2020) sit beside FAIR and say Indigenous communities decide what is shared ([Data Science Journal](https://datascience.codata.org/articles/dsj-2020-043)). BDRC’s policy opens public-domain scans, blocks “sealed texts” at lineage holders’ request, and will not waive third-party copyright ([BDRC](https://www.bdrc.io/access-policies/)).

[CLAIM] In January 2026 former BDRC employee Gangkar Lhamo alleged in *Tibet Times* that BDRC was transferring its archive to Harvard–Yenching and dismissing long-term Tibetan staff. Dawa Lokyitsang amplified this as dispossession of heritage Gene Smith wanted kept in Tibetan hands ([Lhakar Diaries, 30 Jan 2026](https://lhakardiaries.com/2026/01/30/the-buddhist-digital-resource-center-transfers-tibetan-buddhist-heritage-to-harvard-dismisses-tibetan-staff/)). The original article and any BDRC response were not independently read; treat the transfer as unverified. [SPECULATION] An outside OSS project that scraped PRC or monastic scans without lineage consent would inherit that critique with none of BDRC’s staffing or trust capital.

### 4. Restricted content

[FACT] Lamdre (*lam ’bras*) is the Sakya tantric core, built on Hevajra. Uncommon manuals stayed in manuscript until Jamyang Loter Wangpo printed a 17-volume *Lamdre Lobshey* in 1904 against warnings that printing would anger protectors ([catalogue essay](https://sacred-texts.com/bud/tib/sakya-la.htm)). Wisdom still sells Ngorchen’s *Ornament to Beautify the Three Continua* as restricted: the Sakya Trichen’s foreword requires the Hevajra initiation ([Wisdom](https://wisdomexperience.org/product/the-preliminary-and-main-practices-of-the-sakya-lamdre-tradition/)).

[FACT] 84000, after consulting senior teachers, publishes Kangyur tantras **without** access control, with a warning that Vajrayāna has restrictions ([Canti / 84000](https://84000.co/post/tantra-kangyur-section)). Lotsawa House follows Alak Zenkar Rinpoche: publish almost everything, warn, label a few texts restricted ([Lotsawa House](https://www.lotsawahouse.org/about/restricted-texts-policy)). BDRC goes further on sealed works: *The Secret Heart Essence* (W1CZ1073) is public domain but “we completely block any access… except from specific accounts.” [SPECULATION] Open MT of Lamdre pith instructions or Hevajra sadhanas is the class Sakya authorities would refuse. Kangyur sūtras, history, medicine, and “three appearances” preliminaries are the class they have already allowed into print.

### 5. Political risk

[FACT] Lobsang Thapkhey, former librarian of Kirti Monastery (Ngaba, Sichuan), was arrested in June 2023 and sentenced to three years for importing Buddhist philosophy books from India and for offerings to the Dalai Lama. The books had no political content ([TCHRD, 19 Sep 2024](https://tchrd.org/2024/09/19/tibetan-librarian-at-kirti-monastery-sentenced-to-three-years-in-chinese-prison/)). That is the precedent for “helping a monastery library from abroad.”

[FACT] The *Measures for the Administration of Internet Religious Information Services* (in force 1 Mar 2022) require a licence to publish religious doctrine, knowledge, or activities to the public via websites, apps, WeChat, or livestreams **inside the PRC**. Unlicensed persons may not preach online or livestream rituals ([State Council Gazette](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5678093.htm)). US reporting treats enforcement as an effective ban on monks offering religious content online ([2024 Country Reports — Tibet](https://www.state.gov/reports/2024-country-reports-on-human-rights-practices/china/tibet)). TAR residents are surveilled for Dalai Lama images and for contact with people outside Tibet on sensitive dates.

[SPECULATION] Naming TAR monastic collaborators, accepting exfiltrated scans, or framing the work as “liberating texts the CCP hides” would create a fileable case against those monks (separatism or unlicensed religious information). Risk to overseas contributors without TAR family is mainly copyright/ToS and reputation. Risk to the monastery is political if a foreign project uses its name without the management committee.

### 6. Is the Sakya-specific corpus reachable?

**Partly.** Reachable: Kathmandu IBA/Sachen e-texts (CC BY-NC-SA), BDRC public-domain scans of already-circulating Sakya collected works, Library of Tibetan Classics volumes issued with Sakya Trizin approval, 84000 and Lotsawa House translations. Not reachable: on-site photography of the scripture wall; the monastery’s unpublished page-scans; any dump that requires a PRC collaborator to exfiltrate files. “Partly” is a design constraint: the OSS surface is the *already published* Sakya literature, not the 84,000-volume tourist statistic.

## Gaps and what would settle them

- **Are the monastery page-scans on any server a non-Chinese user can hit?** A URL or IIIF manifest would settle this. Until then, treat “digitized” as “locally archived.”
- **Reconcile 2022 “10,000 volumes / 20%” with 2025 “3,000 volumes / 7,000 pages.”** A current leaf/volume count from Luo Zhuo Jia Cuo or Kunga Trinley.
- **Did CTRC’s Tibetan literature data centre ever leave the intranet?** Access tests from outside and inside the PRC.
- **Gangkar Lhamo / BDRC / Harvard.** Read the 28 Jan 2026 *Tibet Times* article and any BDRC statement. The ethics debate does not wait on that fact; the legal-theft claim does.
- **Any 2020–2026 foreign-scholar permit to *work* in a TAR monastic library.** None found. A published field report would overturn this.
- **PRC court file for Lobsang Thapkhey.** TCHRD is the only source located.

## Implication for the open-source project

- **AGAINST sourcing from TAR Sakya itself.** Permits, photography bans, relic law, and unpublished scans make on-site or leaked-scan acquisition a non-starter. Design as if the wall does not exist as a data source.
- **FOR working with already-published Sakya literature under explicit licences.** IBA/Sachen CC BY-NC-SA, BDRC public-domain scans, 84000, and Sakya Trizin–approved print volumes are the legal corpus. Honour NC/ND; do not relicense as unrestricted MIT.
- **AGAINST scraping 中华古籍资源库, CTRC, or Tibet Library as a “Sakya dump.”** Those platforms are token Tibetan holdings or intranet. Scraping them collides with 信息网络传播权 and CARE.
- **AGAINST unsupervised MT of Lamdre/Hevajra practice texts.** Mirror 84000’s warning model for canonical tantras if lineage advisors agree; keep a sealed bucket, as BDRC does, for pith instructions.
- **AGAINST recruiting or naming TAR monastic collaborators.** The Kirti librarian case is the constraint. If the project needs Tibetan authority, use exile Sakya institutions that can consent in public.
- **NEUTRAL-to-FOR a lineage-advised translation layer on texts already on the open web.** That is a different project from “unlocking 84,000 hidden manuscripts,” and the README should say so.
