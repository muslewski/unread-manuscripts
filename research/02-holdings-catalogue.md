# What the Sakya library actually holds, and what is catalogued

## Verdict

- **84,000 is a wall-stack estimate, not a title count.** [FACT] Feb 2022, monastery committee to China News Service: **>50,000 volumes** (*5万余部*), **21 palm-leaf MSS**; **internal cataloguing finished 2020**; ~10,000 vols (everything except the assembly-hall wall) scanned leaf-by-leaf. ([TAR C&T reprint](https://wlt.xizang.gov.cn/xwzx_69/wlyw/dsdt/202202/t20220228_286433.html))
- **Most of it is not unknown literature.** [FACT] Named wall contents: Kangyur, Tengyur, deluxe Prajñāpāramitā, Lamdré, *Tshad ma rigs gter*, *Sa skya bka' 'bum*, throne-holder biographies, plus medicine/history/law/calendar — already printed and catalogued elsewhere. ([CCTV 2009](http://tibet.cctv.com/20090112/110944.shtml); [China News 2024](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2024/10-27/10308965.shtml))
- **Sanskrit at Sakya is real but tiny; TAR Sanskrit is a closed academic program.** [FACT] TAR survey 2006–11: ~1,000 bundles / ~60,000 folios / ~3,500–4,000 texts; originals locked; CTRC–Vienna publish from photocopies. Sakya palm-leaf count: **21**. ([Steinkellner 2020](https://www.austriaca.at/0xc1aa5572_0x003df50d.pdf))
- **No published scholarly catalogue of the monastery, unlike Drepung.** [FACT] Drepung: printed *哲蚌寺藏古籍目录* (Minzu Press, 2004, 2,483 pp.). Sakya’s 2020 catalogue is an internal monastic database. No print or open OPAC found.
- **Scholarly-novel range: ~1–8% of volumes as unicum *works*; ~10–30% as unique *witnesses* of known works.** [SPECULATION] “95% of 84,000 untranslated” confuses volume-count, language, and uniqueness. See §5.

## Findings

### 1. Composition breakdown

**The numbers.** [FACT] Older copy: ~44,000–45,000 volumes in the Lhakhang Chenmo wall (CCTV 2009); still older room tallies ~24,000 *pecha* across three stores. [CLAIM] 2003 Xinhua/TASS popularised “as many as 84,000 scrolls” in a 60×10 m wall, “untouched for hundreds of years.” [FACT] 84,000 is also a Buddhist sacred number (84,000 dharma-doors), not a counted inventory. **>50,000 volumes (2022)** is the only figure attached to a finished catalogue. Wikipedia’s “84,000 books, all catalogued as of 2022” conflates estimate with scan report.

**Kangyur / Tengyur.** [FACT] Derge Kangyur ~108 vols / ~1,100 titles; Derge Tengyur ~213 vols / ~3,350 titles ([Derge Parkhang](http://www.degeparkhang.org/cn/sutra/11.html); [84000 tables](https://84000.co/kb-articles/kangyur-tengyur-genres-number-of-texts-pages-and-text-size)). [FACT] The wall holds Kangyur/Tengyur copies, including Yuan gold/silver deluxe MSS under 'Phags pa. Several deluxe sets plus extra *Sher phyin* copies produce five-figure *volume* counts without new titles.

**Sakya-school literature.** [FACT] The *Sa skya bka' 'bum* of the Five Founders is a **15-volume Derge xylograph (1736)**, reprinted, fully in BDRC ([W22271](https://library.bdrc.io/show/bdr:MW22271)), with a title catalogue (Hochotsang, Gangtok 1990) and a school bibliography (Khenpo Appey; Jackson, *IIJ* 34, 1991). Lamdré, Ngor, and Tsarpa collected works were likewise xylographed at Derge. [FACT] The 230-odd-volume *Sakya Gapen Treasury* (85 fascicles 2025) gathers already-identified throne-holder writings from global fragments, not the wall ([China Daily 14 Apr 2025](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202504/14/WS67fcec24a3104d9fd381f2fd.html)).

**Admin / history / medicine / astrology.** [FACT] Older descriptions put ~3,000 vols of astronomy, calendar, medicine, literature, history in the northern “Gurong” store — destroyed with North Sakya in the Cultural Revolution. The 2022 statement still lists politics, law, history, calendar, medicine among the scanned remainder. [CLAIM] Unique local records vs copies of the *Rgyud bzhi*, *Vaidūrya dkar po*, *Sa skya gdung rabs*, published Yuan seals: not public.

**Unicum.** [FACT] A handful of Sakya items are on the *National Precious Ancient Books List* (Yuan gold 'Phags pa sūtra MS; Ming Grags pa rgyal mtshan Lamdré MS) ([China Daily 2014 list](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/dfpd/xz/2014-12/11/content_19066671.htm)). [SPECULATION] Profile of a famous monastic library: deluxe recensions and a few local treasures, not a sealed cave. The “library discovered behind a wall in 2003” story is press misunderstanding of the already-famous scripture wall being surveyed in the 2002 restoration.

### 2. Sanskrit palm-leaf manuscripts

**TAR-wide, not Sakya-wide.** [FACT] 2006–11 palm-leaf survey (Hu Jintao order; Tsewang Gyurme / TASS): **~1,000 bundles, ~60,000 folios**. Steinkellner infers **~3,500–4,000 texts/fragments**. 80% of palm-leaf plus paper MSS colour-digitised. Output: DVDs + **61 portfolio vols + 4-vol general catalogue, five copies, not sold**. 2016: still “out of bounds for scholars, both within China and beyond” ([Steinkellner in *Sanskrit Manuscripts in China III*, 2020, pp. 9–16](https://www.austriaca.at/0xc1aa5572_0x003df50d.pdf)).

**Sakya specifically.** [FACT] 2022: **21 palm-leaf MSS**, dated by the committee to the 13th c. [CLAIM] Older copy: 8 (CCTV 2009) or “originally 100+, now 21” ([Phoenix 2008](https://fo.ifeng.com/chaosheng/200811/1110_19_52094_3.shtml)) — the 100+ figure is unverified. [FACT] Some Sakya Sanskrit MSS moved to Lhasa 1962; Luo Zhao’s unpublished Norbulingka catalogue lists two brought from Sakya 18 Aug 1976 (Kano 2015). [FACT] Steinkellner in 1999 was shown at Sakya the paper *Pramāṇavārttikālaṅkāra* in Vibhūticandra’s hand, previously photographed by Sāṅkṛtyāyana (*A Tale of Leaves*, 2004).

**Who catalogued, who has access.** [FACT] Luo Zhao unpublished catalogues 1984–85 (Norbulingka, Potala, Drepung appendix, Lhoka). Wang Sen listed 259 Sanskrit MSS then at the China Ethnic Library. Sāṅkṛtyāyana 1934–38 listed ~363 items in *JBORS* (Sakya, Ngor, Zhwa lu); Göttingen has Bandurski 1994 of his photos; Tucci photographed overlapping material. [FACT] Jan 2004 CTRC–Austrian Academy agreement opened **photocopies at CTRC Beijing**, not originals. STTAR: ~23–26 critical editions (Steinkellner, Krasser, Li Xuezhu, Luo Hong 罗鸿, etc.). Panels: *Sanskrit Manuscripts in China* I–III (2008, 2012, 2016). [FACT] Sāṅkṛtyāyana called **Ngor** richer in subject-matter; many Ngor/Zhwa lu MSS had come from Sakya.

**Edited vs locked.** [FACT] A few dozen TAR Sanskrit texts have STTAR/journal editions. The 2011 4-vol general catalogue is not a circulating book. [SPECULATION] Of Sakya’s 21 palm-leaf items, some will match Sāṅkṛtyāyana/Tucci photos already studied; some may be unique. Uncountable without Luo/TASS/2011 catalogues.

### 3. Published catalogues

[FACT] 2020: monks finished cataloguing (date, author, domain) and built a title database plus a full-text database of the scanned 10,000 vols. I found **no** printed *萨迦寺古籍目录*, no BDRC work matching a complete Sakya-monastery shelf-list, no NLC/TAR Library public OPAC of the wall.

What exists: traditional *Sa skya dkar chag* (Tucci/Filibeck) — architecture/relics; Luo Zhao 1984–85 — unpublished Sanskrit; TASS 2003 survey — no catalogue found; Hochotsang 1990 — Kabum only; Potala *pho brang po ta la'i sa skya'i gsung rab dkar chag* (2015, 621 pp., [BDRC MW3CN5011](https://library.bdrc.io/show/bdr:MW3CN5011)) — **Sakya-school texts at the Potala**, not the monastery; Pal tsegs Drepung 2004 — public; Sakya 2011–20 database — not public.

[FACT] TAR *Zhonghua guji* census (from 2009) had ~13,700 fascicles by 2019, with Sakya still **pending** ([China Culture Daily](http://culture.ycwb.com/2019-02/20/content_30201747.htm)). The 2020 self-catalogue and the national census are different instruments; neither is a Drepung-style published title list.

### 4. Comparable collections

**Drepung.** [FACT] Pal tsegs (百慈 / *dpal brtsegs*) 2002–04 archived ~10,000 pre-17th-c. vols at Drepung, then catalogued ~30,000 vols and published *哲蚌寺藏古籍目录* (2004, 2 vols, 2,483 pp., ISBN 9787105066902) ([Sina 2011](http://collection.sina.com.cn/gjsb/20110825/143936574.shtml)). Unique residue was then printed as the 120-vol *bKa' gdams gsung 'bum*. The brief’s “~40,000 titles” is the same order as Pal tsegs’ ~30,000 *volumes*; I found no reliable 40,000-title count. **A catalogued Tibetan monastic library is a printed dkar chag plus published residue.** Sakya has not had that public step.

**Potala.** [FACT] Sanskrit: Luo Zhao unpublished. Sakya-school Tibetan: 2015 printed catalogue. Deluxe Kangyurs are known objects.

**Narthang.** [FACT] Historic Kangyur/Tengyur printer. Sakya’s wall copies sit downstream of that print culture (and Derge).

**Dunhuang / IDP.** [FACT] Closed 5th–11th-c. corpus. IDP as of 1 Jan 2026: 154,919 catalogue entries, 114,332 digitised. Tibetan: BL ~3,100 Dunhuang Tibetan scrolls + ~2,300 woodslips; Pelliot tibétain 4,174; Gansu pothi leaves in the thousands. Catalogues exist. Uniqueness is high because of *date* and secular documents, not because the cave is uncatalogued. Sakya is later (mostly 13th–18th c. copies), larger in cubic metres, and **worse published**. “Second Dunhuang” is a heritage slogan, not a claim about unknown centuries.

### 5. Scholarly-novel fraction

Volume ≠ title. 50,000 stacked *pecha* can be a few thousand titles with heavy duplication. “Not in English” ≠ unknown (84000.org; Lotsawa House / IBA). “Not on the internet” ≠ uncatalogued (Derge prints sit in BDRC).

**Unicum *works* never in BDRC, never edited:** [SPECULATION] **~1–8% of volumes, perhaps a few hundred to low-thousands of titles.** Pal tsegs’ Drepung harvest yielded one major unpublished corpus (Kadam collected works) plus ordinary canonical duplication. Remaining unique titles likeliest in: unpublished later Sakya/Ngor/Tsarpa *gsung 'bum*; local histories and legal documents; a subset of the 21 Sanskrit MSS. North Sakya’s libraries were destroyed.

**Unique *witnesses* (textual-critical value, work known):** [SPECULATION] **~10–30% of volumes.** Yuan gold/silver MSS, old *dbu med* copies, and Sanskrit palm-leaves can change editions of texts we already have. That is philology, not a lost civilisation.

**Not plausible:** 95% of 84,000 items as unknown untranslated literature. That fails on count, duplication, canon, catalogues, and editions.

## Gaps and what would settle them

1. **2020 Sakya catalogue not public.** Settled by a scan/print of the title database or a Pal tsegs-style dkar chag. Until then composition percentages are inference from named genres.
2. **Luo Zhao 1984–85 and the 2011 four-volume Sanskrit general catalogue remain restricted.** Settled by release, or a concordance of the 21 palm-leaf items against Sāṅkṛtyāyana / Bandurski / Wang Sen.
3. **Volume-to-title ratio of the wall unknown.** Settled by one sampled bay fully listed (title, author, date, Derge-equivalent or not).
4. **North Sakya’s Uze/Gurong libraries after the Cultural Revolution not itemised.** Settled by restoration reports or the TASS 2003 internal survey.
5. **Overlap with BDRC** cannot be computed without the 2020 list. Settled by matching that list against BDRC work-IDs.

## Implication for the open-source project

- **AGAINST treating Sakya as an uncatalogued lost library.** The monastery has catalogued itself; the canon is in 84000 / Derge / BDRC; the Kabum is scanned; TAR Sanskrit is already CTRC–Vienna editions with no public images.
- **AGAINST designing around “84,000 untranslated books.”** Design around ~50,000 *volumes* of mostly known works, a minority of unique witnesses, and ~21 Sanskrit MSS you will not photograph.
- **FOR a narrow philological niche only if** the 2020 catalogue shows unpublished later Sakya *gsung 'bum* or local historical documents **and** those can be legally sourced. Archival partnership, not AI-translation-of-the-unknown.
- **AGAINST Sanskrit as an OSS target.** The unmet need is political access to TAR originals. Photocopy editions are already STTAR’s job.
- **Drepung lesson:** the productive move was publishing a catalogue, then printing the unique residue. An OSS project that cannot do that is downstream of work China already claims internally.
- **If it proceeds anyway,** the honest object is open editions of already-public Sakya xylographs and BDRC scans, not the wall. Do not sell that as unlocking 95% of 84,000 hidden books.
